Executive Summary

Fraud detection is an extreme class-imbalance problem. This project evaluates whether synthetic oversampling can improve detection of rare fraudulent transactions.

Headline result: SMOTE increases fraud recall from 59.2% to 89.8%, reducing missed fraud from 40 cases to 10.

Dataset Overview

284,807 transactions were analyzed: 284,315 genuine (99.827%) and 492 fraudulent (0.173%). Accuracy alone is misleading because a model can classify almost everything as genuine and still exceed 99% accuracy.

Operational Impact

Trade-off: SMOTE reduces missed fraud by 75% but increases false-positive alerts from 9 to 115.

Why Precision Dropped — and How to Improve It

Synthetic fraud examples broaden the representation of the minority class. The model becomes more sensitive to fraud-like patterns, increasing recall while also increasing false positives.

Final Recommendation

SMOTE provides strong evidence that augmentation can materially improve fraud capture. The next priority is to improve the precision-recall balance through threshold tuning and stronger models before controlled pilot deployment.

Key takeaway: The goal is not simply to maximize recall. The goal is to minimize expected business loss while keeping investigation workload manageable.
